The Energy Goes to Speaking, Not Seeing

We decompose VLM inference energy into prefill (processing all inputs) and decode (generating output tokens). Across all 5 models and 2 hardware platforms:

Autoregressive decoding accounts for 86–97% of total energy. The compute-bound prefill phase—which processes all visual tokens—is a minority contributor.

Key implication: Even removing all visual tokens saves at most 10% of total energy for fixed-token models. Controlling output length saves up to 97%.

The Counterintuitive Truth

Existing VLM efficiency work focuses on reducing visual tokens. Our measurements reveal this targets the wrong bottleneck.

👁️

  • Power is resolution-invariant (<5% CV)
  • 14× more visual tokens → no measurable power change
  • Content complexity doesn't affect power draw
  • Prompt type doesn't change power
  • Prefill is compute-bound, fast, and cheap

Speaking is Not

  • Each output token costs 11–39× more than input
  • Decode dominates 86–97% of total energy

  • Image complexity → 4.1× energy variation (via output length)
  • Decode is memory-bound, sequential, and expensive
  • Output length control: up to 97% energy savings

Key Findings

Three contributions from systematic energy profiling across 5 VLMs, 4 resolutions, and 2 edge platforms.

Finding 1

Power is a Model Fingerprint

Average inference power is a model-intrinsic constant, invariant to input resolution, image complexity, and prompt type, with less than 5% variation. Energy analysis reduces to understanding inference time.

Finding 2

Decode Dominates Everything

Autoregressive decoding accounts for 86–97% of total energy. The decode-to-prefill cost ratio per token ranges from 11× to 39×, confirming the compute-bound vs. memory-bound architectural asymmetry.

Finding 3

Visual Token Pruning — An Illusion

We derive a theoretical upper bound: removing all visual tokens saves at most 10% of total energy for fixed-token models. By contrast, controlling output length saves 2–24× more energy.

Finding 4

Output Length is the Energy Lever

Reducing output from 398 to 3 tokens cuts energy by 90% while prefill energy stays unchanged. Output token count is the first-order predictor of per-inference energy.

Finding 5

Cross-Model Energy Predictor

A single linear model with 5 features (model size, input/output counts, and interactions) achieves R²=0.986 and MAPE=10.3% across all 1,680 runs—without per-model calibration.

Finding 6

Content Affects Energy via Output

Image complexity induces up to 4.1× energy differences at identical resolution—not from visual processing cost, but entirely through its effect on output length.

Deployment Guidelines

Three actionable strategies for energy-constrained VLM deployment, derived from our energy analysis.

🎯

Budget Output, Not Input

Each output token costs 11–39× more than each input token. Setting a lower max_tokens is the single most effective energy control. Reducing from 256 to 128 tokens saves ~40–50% energy across all models.

🎭

Match Strategy to Task

If the application requires high-resolution input (e.g., fine-grained visual inspection), use fixed-token architectures (InternVL3, Gemma-3) to avoid the resolution-dependent energy penalty of dynamic-token models.

🔋

Anticipate Content-Driven Variation

Image content causes up to 4.1× energy variation through output length. For battery budgeting in embodied agents, use worst-case max_tokens rather than average output length. Our universal predictor enables such estimation.

The Energy Equation

VLM inference energy is low-dimensional and architecturally determined.

E ≈ P · (αp · Nin + αd · Nout + β)
P = power fingerprint (model-intrinsic constant)
αp = prefill cost per token (cheap)
αd = decode cost per token (11–39× more)
Nout = output length (the dominant driver)
